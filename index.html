Why NutriMatch Was Created

For years, I was trapped in a frustrating cycle with doctors who wouldn't listen. I was handed a "garbage can" diagnosis of IBS—which I believe is one of the most overdiagnosed conditions in modern medicine—along with chronic excess acid.

Before long, I was put on a running prescription for Omeprazole, a harsh medication with severe long-term side effects. Between the symptoms and the side effects, I couldn’t even keep a full-time job. I knew I had reached a breaking point: something had to change.

I started monitoring everything I ate, but it felt like everything made me react. Desperate for answers, I took a food allergy test and cut out every trigger food. Almost instantly, my energy levels soared. That breakthrough sparked an obsession with deep research into food science, cellular bioavailability, and targeted exercise.

By rebuilding my diet from the ground up, I balanced my hormones, healed my gut, and got my life back. NutriMatch was born to save you from the years of trial, error, and medical dismissal I went through—giving you direct, exact pathways to food as true healing.

Gut-Brain Axis (Free Preview) Published October 2026

Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs) and Neuro-Inflammation

Recent findings highlight how fermentable fibers from legumes and cooked/cooled resistant starches yield high concentrations of butyrate (a beneficial compound produced by gut bacteria that acts like natural spackle to repair holes in your gut wall). Butyrate directly influences gut lining integrity, reducing systemic low-grade inflammation (body-wide irritation) often misdiagnosed as functional acid reflux or IBS.

📚 Scientific Reference: Bourassa et al., "Butyrate, neuroepigenetics and the gut-brain axis," Neuroscience Letters (2016); updated in Gut Microbiome & Neuro-Inflammation Reviews (2026).
💡 What This Means & How to Use It:

The Benefit: Feeds good gut bacteria to rebuild leaky intestinal walls, calming digestion issues like bloating, acid reflux, and brain fog.

How to Implement: Cook potatoes, rice, or oats, let them cool down in the fridge overnight, and eat them cold or gently reheated. Cooling converts the starch into gut-healing fiber fuel.

Nutrient Synergies Published September 2026

Piperine and Curcumin Bioavailability Enhancement

Clinical trials confirm that co-administering piperine (the active compound in black pepper) with curcumin (the active anti-inflammatory compound in turmeric) inhibits hepatic glucuronidation (the liver's natural flushing process that normally clears nutrients out before your cells can use them), raising serum curcumin bioavailability (absorption rate into your bloodstream) by up to 2,000%.

📚 Scientific Reference: Shoba et al., "Influence of piperine on the pharmacokinetics of curcumin," Planta Medica; validated in Clinical Phytotherapy (2026).
💡 What This Means & How to Use It:

The Benefit: Supercharges turmeric's natural anti-inflammatory powers to relieve joint pain, gut inflammation, and muscle soreness.

How to Implement: Whenever cooking with ground or fresh turmeric (in teas, soups, or golden milk), always add a tiny pinch of freshly cracked black pepper and a splash of healthy fat (like olive or coconut oil) to lock in maximum absorption.

Micronutrient Timing Published August 2026

Magnesium Glycinate vs. Citrate for Sleep & Acid Sensitivity

Comparative study indicates that highly chelated magnesium glycinate (magnesium bound to the calming amino acid glycine) minimizes gastrointestinal motility irritation (stomach cramping, diarrhea, and digestive rush) compared to oxide or citrate forms, making it optimal for individuals prone to gastric sensitivity.

📚 Scientific Reference: Siebrecht, S., "Magnesium Bisglycinate: Bioavailability and Tolerance in Gastrointestinal Disorders," Journal of Applied Nutritional Science (2026).
💡 What This Means & How to Use It:

The Benefit: Deeply relaxes tense muscles and promotes restful sleep without triggering acid reflux, stomach cramps, or emergency trips to the bathroom.

How to Implement: Take Magnesium Glycinate roughly 30–60 minutes before bed alongside a small glass of water, or focus on dietary magnesium like raw pumpkin seeds (30g) or almonds in the evening.
